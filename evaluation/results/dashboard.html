How are leakages detected and localized using the reconstructor and the predictor?
Leakages are detected and localized by analyzing the discrepancies or residuals between the outputs of the reconstructor and the predictor [C1], [C50], [C30]. Specifically, the process works as follows: 1. The reconstructor estimates the actual nodal pressures using current measurements (time step $t$), while the predictor predicts the leak-free nodal pressures using previous measurements (time steps $t-T, \dots, t-1$) [C30], [C47]. 2. The residual pressure is calculated by comparing the outputs of these two networks [C30]. 3. Because leakages occur at edges while residual signals are on nodes, the absolute edge residual is computed as $r_{uv}^{(E)}(t) = |r_v(t) - r_u(t)|$ [C47]. 4. The moving average of this edge residual over a window size $s$ is calculated [C47]. Since normal residual signals are mainly caused by random noise and exhibit stationary behavior, deviations of the residual signals from this stationary behavior imply abnormal system behavior (leakages) [C47]. 5. Data from a prior period (e.g., 2018) is used to determine a threshold $\xi$ for residual analysis to evaluate performance and locate leakages [C50].
Reference: A reconstructor estimates the actual pressures from current measurements and a predictor estimates leak-free pressures from previous measurements. Leakages are detected and localized by analyzing the discrepancies (residuals) between the two outputs.
